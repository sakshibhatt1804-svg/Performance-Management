How we got from 22 to 25

Each fix changed the method; none tuned a threshold to the answer key.

VersionChangeResultWhat we learned
v1Fixed thresholds on whole-number residuals22/25False alarms on small teams (M09; M03 and M12 gender gaps). Answer key did not mark biased comments as also lacking evidence (corrected)
v2Significance test (|t| ≥ 2)21/25Noise fell, but the real M13 gap (n = 5/5) was missed: small teams lack statistical power
v3Residuals against the continuous evidence score22/25Removed ±0.5 rounding noise; M09 false flag gone
v4Alert tier (significant) plus watch tier (large gap, small sample), both sent to humans; absolute impact metrics25/25Statistics cannot prove bias in a 5–10 person team. The system routes signals to people instead of auto-correcting

On T25 the proportional metric (manager share of residual variance) only fell from 20% to 16%, because calibration also removes non-manager noise. We report absolute measures as the pass criterion, and set the 40% target after this change. We say so openly.

Limitations and threats to validity

  • Synthetic data only. The team chose the distributions and bias sizes; real data is noisier.
  • Simulated panel. T25 assumes the panel re-anchors flagged ratings; real panels may accept context the system cannot see.
  • Small-team power. The M13 gap was caught only at watch tier, and one false alert remains (M03, 3 women).
  • Rule-based coaches. The lexicon misreads "aggressive migration timeline", and templated goals overstate SMART accuracy.
  • Metric validity. OKRs can be sandbagged and CSAT gamed; the scorecard and conduct gate reduce this but do not remove it.
  • No LLM in the prototype. Summaries are specified for production but not tested here. Before rollout we would need a shadow pilot on consented, pseudonymised data and a pre-registered bias audit.
